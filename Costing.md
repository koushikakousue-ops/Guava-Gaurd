# Costing and Feasibility

## Cost Considerations

Guava Guard is designed as a low-cost and sustainable fruit-packaging solution using agricultural waste materials.

### Major Cost Components

- Rice husk biochar
- Banana pseudostem cellulose
- Tapioca starch binder
- Processing and fabrication
- Packaging

## Cost Optimization

Rice husk and banana pseudostem are low-cost agricultural residues. Tapioca starch is also inexpensive and locally available. Local sourcing and bulk processing can further reduce the production cost.

The final cost depends on material quantity, sheet size, thickness, processing method and production scale.

## Target Market

- Small-scale farmers
- Fruit traders
- Wholesalers and retailers
- Fruit transporters
- Small agricultural supply chains

## Feasibility

Guava Guard can be used with existing fruit crates without electricity or refrigeration. The use of rice husk biochar, banana pseudostem cellulose and tapioca starch makes it affordable, biodegradable and suitable for rural applications.

## Limitations

The prototype has not yet experimentally confirmed exact ethylene adsorption capacity or shelf-life extension. Further testing is required to optimize the formulation, production cost and commercial feasibility.
