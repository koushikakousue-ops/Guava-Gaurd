# Guava Guard – Project Documentation

## 1. Project Overview

Guava Guard is a proposed low-cost, biodegradable multilayer liner designed to be placed inside existing guava crates during transportation and short-term storage.

The system aims to delay guava ripening and reduce post-harvest deterioration by creating a passive micro-environment around the fruit without requiring electricity, refrigeration, sensors or complex operation.

## 2. Problem Statement

Guava is a highly perishable climacteric fruit. During transportation and storage, ripening is accelerated by ethylene accumulation, moisture and mechanical damage.

Small-scale farmers may not have easy access to refrigerated storage or advanced post-harvest technologies. Therefore, an affordable and simple solution that can work with existing transportation crates is required.

## 3. Proposed Solution

Guava Guard is designed as a multilayer liner containing:

1. Ethylene-scavenging layer
2. Porous cellulose-based layer
3. Protective/support layer

The liner is designed to fit inside an existing fruit crate and function passively during transportation.

## 4. Working Principle

Guava naturally releases ethylene during ripening.

The proposed ethylene-scavenging layer uses rice-husk-derived biochar as an adsorption material. The biochar is intended to interact with ethylene present in the micro-environment surrounding the fruit.

A porous cellulose-based layer made from agricultural biomass such as banana pseudostem is positioned between the fruit and the scavenging material.

The porous structure is intended to allow gas movement while providing physical separation and mechanical support.

The liner also provides an absorbent surface that can help manage excess moisture inside the package.

## 5. Layer Structure

### Layer 1 – Ethylene Scavenging Layer

Proposed material: Rice husk biochar.

Function:
- Adsorption of ethylene from the surrounding atmosphere
- Utilization of agricultural waste
- Passive operation without electricity

### Layer 2 – Porous Cellulose Layer

Proposed material: Banana pseudostem-derived cellulose/fibre.

Function:
- Physical separation between fruit and scavenging material
- Porous pathway for gas movement
- Moisture management
- Mechanical support

## 6. Prototype Development

A demonstration prototype was developed using readily available materials to represent the proposed multilayer structure.

The prototype demonstrates:
- Crate-compatible liner geometry
- Separation of functional layers
- Porous layer concept
- Placement of the liner inside a transport container

The prototype is a functional model of the proposed design and does not represent the final material formulation.

## 7. Manufacturing Concept

The proposed manufacturing process is:

Agricultural waste
→ Preparation of rice husk
→ Pyrolysis
→ Rice husk biochar
→ Formation of ethylene-scavenging layer

Banana pseudostem
→ Cleaning
→ Fibre/cellulose extraction
→ Drying
→ Formation of porous cellulose layer

The functional layers are then assembled into a multilayer liner and cut according to the dimensions of the target fruit crate.

## 8. Expected Benefits

- Low-cost post-harvest intervention
- Uses agricultural waste as raw material
- Passive operation
- No electricity required
- No refrigeration required
- Compatible with existing crates
- Potential reduction in ethylene accumulation
- Potential moisture management
- Potential reduction in mechanical damage during transportation
- Suitable for small-scale and local transportation systems

## 9. Limitations

The current prototype demonstrates the physical concept and layer arrangement.

The actual ethylene adsorption capacity, optimum biochar quantity, moisture absorption capacity, shelf-life extension and final cost require laboratory testing and validation.

Therefore, Guava Guard does not claim to completely prevent ripening or eliminate post-harvest losses.

## 10. Future Development

Future work will focus on:

- Optimising biochar concentration and layer thickness
- Measuring ethylene adsorption capacity
- Measuring moisture absorption
- Testing different cellulose-based materials
- Testing liner performance with guava under controlled conditions
- Studying shelf-life extension
- Optimising liner dimensions for different crate sizes
- Conducting biodegradability and safety studies
- Developing a scalable manufacturing process
- Performing field trials with farmers

## 11. Prototype Status

Current status: Proof-of-concept prototype.

The present prototype demonstrates the proposed multilayer architecture and its placement inside a transport crate. Further experimental validation is required before claiming commercial performance.
