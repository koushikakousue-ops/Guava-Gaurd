 # Guava Guard

## Low-Cost Multilayer Liner for Reducing Post-Harvest Deterioration in Guava

###  Overview

Guava Guard is a proposed low-cost, biodegradable multilayer liner designed to be placed inside existing fruit crates to help delay guava ripening and reduce post-harvest deterioration during transportation and short-term storage.

The concept combines an agricultural-waste-derived ethylene-scavenging layer with a porous cellulose-based layer, providing a passive solution that does not require electricity, refrigeration or complex operation.

##  Problem

Guava is a highly perishable climacteric fruit. Rapid ripening, ethylene accumulation, moisture-related deterioration and mechanical damage during transportation can contribute to post-harvest losses.

Small-scale farmers and local supply chains may have limited access to continuous cold-chain infrastructure.

Therefore, there is a need for a simple, affordable and easy-to-use solution that can work with existing transportation systems.


##  Proposed Solution

Guava Guard is designed as a multilayer liner that can be inserted into conventional guava crates.

### Proposed layers

**1. Rice-Husk Biochar Layer**
- Proposed as the ethylene-scavenging layer.
- Biochar would be produced from rice husk through pyrolysis.
- Intended to adsorb ethylene surrounding the fruit.

**2. Porous Cellulose Layer**
- Proposed using agricultural biomass such as banana pseudostem.
- Provides physical separation between the scavenging material and fruit.
- Allows gas movement through the structure.
- Intended to contribute to moisture management.


##  How It Works

Guava naturally produces ethylene during ripening.

The proposed biochar layer is intended to reduce ethylene accumulation around the fruit through adsorption.

The porous cellulose layer allows gas movement while providing physical separation and structural support.

Together, the layers are intended to create a more favourable micro-environment around guava during transportation.



##  Prototype

A physical proof-of-concept prototype was developed using readily available materials to demonstrate the proposed multilayer structure.

The current demonstration model uses substitute materials and **does not represent the final optimized material formulation**.

The final product is proposed to use rice-husk-derived biochar and a suitable cellulose-based material.



##  Target Users

- Small-scale farmers
- Local fruit traders
- Agricultural transporters
- Short-distance fruit supply chains


##  Key Advantages

- Low-cost concept
- No electricity required
- No sensors or electronics required
- Compatible with existing crates
- Simple to use
- Potential use of agricultural waste
- Designed for decentralized/local supply chains


##  Validation Plan

Future experimental testing will evaluate:

- Ethylene adsorption capacity
- Moisture management
- Mechanical strength
- Gas permeability
- Guava quality changes
- Weight loss
- Firmness
- TSS
- Spoilage
- Shelf-life compared with an untreated control


##  Future Scope

1. Optimize rice-husk biochar production.
2. Determine optimum biochar loading.
3. Develop and optimize the cellulose-based layer.
4. Conduct controlled ethylene adsorption studies.
5. Perform guava shelf-life experiments.
6. Optimize liner dimensions and mechanical strength.
7. Conduct pilot-scale transportation trials.
8. Evaluate economic feasibility at larger production scale.


##  Current Limitations

The present prototype is a proof-of-concept model.

Quantitative claims regarding ethylene removal, moisture control and shelf-life extension require controlled experimental validation.

The final material composition and production cost will be optimized during further development.
